The problem with generic scorecards

One checklist can't fit every job.

Many teams score every role on the same handful of criteria: communication, experience, education, teamwork and culture fit. It feels consistent, but it measures a care assistant and an AI engineer with the same ruler.

  • What the job needs gets diluted. The skills that decide success in this role share the points with criteria that barely apply.
  • Experience crowds out potential. Years in post and qualifications count even when the job says no experience is needed.
  • Buzzwords score well. “Excellent communicator” and “team player” earn points without a shred of evidence.
  • Nobody can say why. When a candidate gets a 7 rather than a 5, there's nothing written down to explain it.

      Built for better judgement

      Fairness is designed in, not bolted on.

      Every AmberRain scorecard is built to the same principles, so each candidate is judged on what they can show for the job and nothing else.

      • Job-relevant evidence only. Scorecards are built never to score names, addresses, age, gender, disability, ethnicity, family status or school prestige.
      • Seniority judged against the role. It's based on recent relevant responsibilities, never age, time since graduation or career length.
      • Not mentioned isn't a no. A CV that doesn't mention a licence isn't treated as proof the candidate doesn't have one.
      • Evidence over buzzwords. Concrete examples from any setting count. Unsupported claims like “team player” don't.
      • Same terms for everyone. Changing a scorecard clears its scores, so no two candidates are compared on different criteria.
      • People decide. Scores support your review. AmberRain never rejects a candidate automatically.

      FAQ

      Questions, answered.

      Is the scorecard really different for every job?

      Yes. Each job description gets its own scorecard, built for that role: 4–8 criteria, each with a weighting and a scoring guide. When two roles share a criterion, it's worded and weighted for each job. In the app, you'll see the scorecard called a scoring rubric.

      Can I change the scorecard?

      Yes. Edit the criteria, scoring guide and weightings directly, or describe what you'd like changed and AmberRain rewrites the scorecard. Changing a scorecard clears existing scores, so every candidate is measured the same way.

      How is a score calculated?

      Each criterion is scored from 0 to 4 against its scoring guide and earns its score ÷ 4 × its weighting, so a 3 out of 4 on a 20-point criterion earns 15 points. The points add up to a total out of 100, shown as a score out of 10. If the job states a level, such as entry level, candidates whose recent roles are well above it get a clearly shown adjustment. It's never based on age or years of experience.

      Does AmberRain reject candidates?

      No. AmberRain ranks and explains; your team decides. Scores are there to support a fair, consistent review, not to replace it.

      What happens when a CV doesn't mention something?

      Requirements have a “needs verification” level. A CV that doesn't mention a licence, for example, is marked as needing verification rather than scored as if the candidate doesn't hold one.

      What does the AI do?

      One AI model drafts each scorecard from the job description. A second model, built for structured decisions, then scores every CV against it, giving each criterion a score, a confidence level and the likelihood of each scoring level.
